What is Alzheimer's Disease?

Alzheimer's disease is the most common cause of dementia — a general term for memory loss and other cognitive abilities serious enough to interfere with daily life. It accounts for 60-80% of dementia cases.

The disease is not a normal part of aging. The greatest known risk factor is increasing age, and the majority of people with Alzheimer's are 65 and older. It is a progressive disease, where symptoms gradually worsen over time. In its early stages, memory loss is mild, but with late-stage Alzheimer's, individuals lose the ability to carry on a conversation and respond to their environment.

The OASIS Dataset

Open Access Series of Imaging Studies — Brain MRI Collection

The OASIS (Open Access Series of Imaging Studies) project provides free access to brain imaging datasets aimed at making MRI data available to the scientific community. Our system uses a curated version of this dataset containing 86,437 MRI brain scan images categorized into 4 classes of Alzheimer's severity.

About the Project

This project was developed as an end-to-end machine learning application for Alzheimer's disease detection using brain MRI scans. It combines advanced deep learning techniques with a user-friendly web interface to provide accessible medical image analysis.

Six different neural network architectures were trained and evaluated, including custom CNN, MLP, and four transfer learning models (VGG16, ResNet50, EfficientNetB0, MobileNetV2). The best-performing model is deployed in this web application for real-time predictions.
